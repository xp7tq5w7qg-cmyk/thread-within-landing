Top-Down Raglan Calculator

Start with your washed swatch, choose a neckline, then add your body measurements, preferred ease and lengths. The stitch counts, schematic and instructions will update as you go.

Before you begin

This calculator makes a straight-body raglan with equal front and back sections and no back-neck short rows. Sleeve width follows the raglan calculation, so check the fit notes before casting on and try on before dividing.

Read the construction and measurement notes
  • The basic shape: four raglan lines, equal front and back sections, a straight body and no back-neck short rows.
  • Your neckline: round neck starts flat with a shaped front scoop, then joins with a centre-front cast-on; pick up its neckband last. Boat neck starts in the round from its full cast-on. The neckline tabs change the opening and finishing instructions.
  • Sleeve fit: your upper-arm measurement checks the resulting sleeve width; it does not set that width independently. Read the fit notes and try on before separating the body and sleeves.
  • Lengths: body and sleeve lengths include their ribbing. Raglan depth covers shaping only, excluding the band and set-up.
  • Finishing estimates: ribbing rounds and neckband pickup counts are guides. Check your swatch and the edge of your garment, and adjust pickup spacing for a flat finish.

A little guide to the knitting terms

If an abbreviation has you pausing, you can look it up here. These are the terms used in your pattern, and they’re included when you copy it as text too.

Show abbreviations and what they mean
TermWhat it means
RS — right sideThe side intended to face out when you wear the jumper. In stockinette, this is the smooth side with V-shaped stitches.
WS — wrong sideThe inside of the jumper. In stockinette, this is the bumpy, purl side. When working flat, turn the work between RS and WS rows.
st / stsStitch / stitches. A number such as 40 sts means 40 stitches on the needle.
k / k1Knit / knit 1 stitch. For example, k12 means knit the next 12 stitches.
p / p1Purl / purl 1 stitch. For example, p12 means purl the next 12 stitches.
pm — place markerPut a stitch marker onto the right-hand needle between stitches. It marks a boundary; it is not a stitch.
sl m / slip markerMove the marker from the left-hand needle to the right-hand needle without working it. Keep the yarn in the position needed for the next stitch.
Beginning-of-round markerMarks where one round ends and the next begins. In the boat-neck and off-shoulder set-up, it also serves as the fourth raglan marker.
M1L — make 1 leftIncrease 1 stitch with a left lean: lift the strand between the needles from front to back with the left needle, then knit it through the back loop.
M1R — make 1 rightIncrease 1 stitch with a right lean: lift the strand between the needles from back to front with the left needle, then knit it through the front loop.
k2tog — knit 2 togetherKnit the next 2 stitches together as one. This decreases 1 stitch and leans to the right.
ssk — slip, slip, knitSlip the next 2 stitches knitwise, one at a time, then knit them together through their back loops. This decreases 1 stitch and leans to the left.
1×1 ribAlternate knit 1, purl 1. In the round, keep knitting the knit columns and purling the purl columns.
StockinetteFor this jumper: knit RS rows and purl WS rows when working flat; knit every round when working in the round.
Row / roundA row is worked across flat knitting, then you turn. A round goes all the way around joined stitches without turning.
Work even / plain roundContinue without increases or decreases. Here, purl a WS row even or knit a plain round, as directed.
Cast on / bind offCast on creates new stitches. Bind off (also called cast off) finishes a live edge so the stitches cannot unravel.
Hold stitchesLeave stitches unworked on waste yarn, a spare cable or a stitch holder until you return to them.
Pick up stitchesDraw new loops through an existing fabric edge onto your needle, as for the neckband or underarm. This differs from returning held live stitches to the needle.
EaseThe difference between body and finished garment measurements. Positive ease adds room; negative ease makes the garment smaller than the body measurement.
BlockWash or dampen the finished knitting as appropriate for the yarn, gently arrange it to the intended shape and let it dry. Check your yarn care instructions; do not rely on blocking to fix a substantial sizing error.
